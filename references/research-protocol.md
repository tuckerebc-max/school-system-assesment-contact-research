# Research Protocol

## Purpose

Find current, publicly attributable professional contact information for senior school-system
leaders. Build a database-quality record of who substantively owns each target function.

## Source hierarchy

Use current public sources in this order:

1. Official school-system sources:
   - leadership or cabinet page;
   - superintendent or executive biography;
   - organizational chart;
   - central-office directory;
   - department page;
   - board agenda, minutes, or personnel announcement;
   - official press release;
   - official contact page;
   - current official PDF or public filing.
2. Other authoritative institutional sources:
   - state education agency;
   - county or municipal government;
   - charter authorizer;
   - diocese or parent organization;
   - official board packet;
   - government, university, or institutional partner directory.
3. Corroborating current sources:
   - reputable local journalism;
   - professional association biography;
   - conference speaker biography;
   - government or university partner page.

LinkedIn may provide a lead or secondary confirmation. Do not use it as the sole source for a
leader's identity, title, or email when an official source is available.

Avoid search-result snippets, unverified contact databases, data brokers, automatically generated
email patterns, and stale staff aggregators.

## District identification

Use NCES District ID as the primary key. Confirm the intended system with State District ID,
district name, state, county, city, address, and official domain. Correct an approximate input
name only when an official source supports the correction.

Do not treat identical district names in different states as duplicates.

## Organizational interpretation

1. Match by functional responsibility, not title keywords alone.
2. Preserve the exact official title.
3. Select the most senior systemwide leader with substantive responsibility.
4. When one leader owns two target functions, create two role records and explain the overlap.
5. When responsibility is divided, include the most relevant leaders and explain the division.
6. Do not substitute:
   - a principal for a central-office leader;
   - a board member for an operating executive;
   - a former employee for the current leader;
   - a vendor or consultant for a system employee;
   - an administrative assistant for the functional leader.
7. Interpret charter, private, diocesan, state-operated, and network structures by their actual
   organization rather than public-LEA title conventions.
8. Label interim, acting, incoming, appointed, outgoing, or vacant status explicitly.

## Email verification

Enter a direct email only when the exact address is publicly displayed in a current attributable
source, including:

- official staff directory, biography, department page, board document, official PDF, government
  directory, institutional partner page, or current document issued by the system.

Never:

- infer an address from colleagues' addresses;
- generate firstname/lastname combinations;
- guess initials;
- alter an old address to match a new domain;
- treat valid syntax as proof of publication or activity.

When no direct email is published, search for a verified role-appropriate office address. Store
it only in the office-email field. When neither is available, enter `Not publicly located` and
provide the best official phone/contact route when available.

Use the most direct, current, role-appropriate address when multiple verified emails exist.
Mention credible alternatives in Research Notes. Store only one address per email field.

## Currentness

For each leader:

1. Verify current incumbency as of the research date.
2. Check recent appointments, resignations, interim service, future start dates, announced
   departures, and reorganizations.
3. Prefer the newest dated official source when official pages conflict.
4. Use recent board materials, press releases, or reputable reporting to resolve conflicts.
5. Do not treat an undated page as definitively current when newer evidence suggests otherwise.
6. Record the research completion date in every row.

## Stopping rule

Before assigning `ROLE NOT IDENTIFIED` to a Tier 1 role, search at minimum:

1. official leadership or cabinet page;
2. official staff directory;
3. relevant department page;
4. organizational chart, when available;
5. recent board agendas, minutes, or leadership announcements;
6. at least one targeted web search using the system name and likely title variants.

Use the same workflow for Tier 2 and Tier 3 roles, but do not allow one difficult role to prevent
completion of the rest of the batch.

## Notes and URL hygiene

Keep notes concise but substantive. Explain title-to-function mapping, transition status,
conflicting evidence, distributed responsibility, unavailable email, and research limitations.

Store full underlying URLs, not search pages. Remove avoidable tracking parameters, fragments
used only for analytics, and redirect wrappers when the clean source URL is known.
